Dilapidation reports in Wollongong

Pre- and post-construction dilapidation inspections across the Wollongong local government area, from Helensburgh to Unanderra.

Wollongong is building upward and outward at once. Apartment towers in the CBD and North Wollongong sit on excavated basements next to older walk-ups. Knockdown-rebuilds and duplexes are replacing fibro and brick cottages through Corrimal, Fairy Meadow and Figtree. Each of those jobs puts vibration and ground movement into the houses next door.

A dilapidation report records the condition of those neighbouring properties before work starts. If an owner later says a crack is new, the builder can show exactly what was there, how wide it was, and where.

Why Wollongong sites need careful records

Much of the northern suburbs is built on the slopes below the escarpment. Sloping blocks mean retaining walls, cut-and-fill and stepped footings, and those details are often where existing cracking already sits. Older brick homes near the coast also show weathering, rusting lintels and mortar loss that are easy to mistake for construction damage later.

Parts of the Illawarra also sit within mine subsidence districts. Historic ground movement can leave its own pattern of cracking, which is another reason to have it recorded by an engineer before your works begin.

Common questions

Does Wollongong City Council require dilapidation reports?

Many development consents in Wollongong include a condition requiring a dilapidation report on adjoining properties before demolition or excavation, and sometimes a second report when works finish. Check the conditions in your consent, or send them to us and we'll tell you what's needed.

How quickly can you inspect in Wollongong?

We're based in Wollongong, so we're usually able to offer inspection dates soon after you accept the quote. The main lead time is arranging access with each neighbour.
