Dilapidation reports in Ryde and Hunters Hill

Pre- and post-construction inspections from Macquarie Park's apartment sites to the heritage sandstone homes of the Hunters Hill peninsula.

Ryde and Hunters Hill sit side by side between the Parramatta and Lane Cove rivers, but the building work in each is very different. The City of Ryde is one of Sydney's busiest areas for new housing, led by Macquarie Park. Hunters Hill is mostly heritage conservation area, where the work is careful renovation of old stone homes. Both put neighbouring properties close to excavation, and both benefit from a clear record before work starts.

The area is covered by two councils: the City of Ryde and Hunter's Hill Council. Gladesville is split between them. Each sets its own consent conditions, so send us your dilapidation condition and we'll match the report to it.

What's being built

  • Macquarie Park apartments and mixed-use towers. The NSW Government's Transport Oriented Development rezoning took effect in November 2024, with capacity for about 9,600 new homes near the Macquarie Park, Macquarie University and North Ryde metro stations. Deep basements here sit beside office buildings, existing apartment blocks and older homes in Marsfield and North Ryde.
  • Knockdown-rebuilds and dual occupancies in Eastwood, Denistone, Ryde, West Ryde and East Ryde, often replacing post-war brick homes on suburban blocks with a basement garage or pool close to the boundary.
  • Apartment and townhouse projects around Top Ryde, Meadowbank and Gladesville, and along Victoria Road.
  • Heritage alterations and additions in Hunters Hill, Woolwich and Huntleys Point, where new work is built against or beside nineteenth-century sandstone houses.
  • Waterfront homes, pools and boatsheds on the sloping foreshores of Putney, Tennyson Point, Woolwich and Henley, usually excavated into sandstone.

Ground and site conditions

  • Shale on the ridges. Much of the higher ground through Ryde is Ashfield Shale over Hawkesbury Sandstone. It weathers to clay soils that can shrink and swell with moisture, so homes on these ridges often already show seasonal cracking. Recording it before works is what stops it being blamed on the job next door.
  • Sandstone in the valleys and on the foreshores. Toward the Lane Cove and Parramatta rivers and the creeks between, excavation usually means rock sawing and breaking. Vibration travels through sandstone into neighbouring footings, pools and retaining walls. See our guide to dilapidation reports for pool excavation.
  • The shale and sandstone boundary. Where a site straddles the two, conditions can change across a single basement.
  • Steep river frontages on the Hunters Hill peninsula, where homes, terraces and sea walls step down to the water and the properties above and below the works both deserve a careful record.

Housing stock we inspect

Hunter's Hill Council describes the municipality as Australia's oldest garden suburb, and more than 70% of it is declared conservation area. Many of its houses built from the 1860s to the First World War are locally quarried sandstone, often with lime mortar, timber floors and lath-and-plaster ceilings. Fine cracking, weathered stone and old repairs are common, and need to be described carefully so they aren't confused with new damage.

Across Ryde you'll find interwar and post-war brick bungalows, 1960s and 1970s walk-up apartment blocks, newer project homes and duplexes, and modern towers in Macquarie Park. Older walk-ups and full-brick homes beside a new basement are often the most sensitive neighbours. If demolition is part of the job, see dilapidation reports for demolition.

Common questions

Do you inspect heritage homes in Hunters Hill?

Yes. Sandstone walls, original plaster, cornices, chimneys and joinery are recorded in detail, because older stone and lime-mortar construction often already has cracking and weathering that needs to be clearly described before work starts next door.

Can you cover strata buildings beside Macquarie Park developments?

Yes. For apartment buildings we inspect the common property closest to the works, such as basements, car parks, driveways and boundary walls, along with any individual units the consent or the builder requires.

Can the report be matched to my City of Ryde or Hunter's Hill Council consent?

Yes. Send us the dilapidation condition from your consent or certificate and we'll make sure the properties, structures and timing it names are covered.
