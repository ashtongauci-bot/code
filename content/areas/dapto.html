Dapto is two building markets side by side. In the established suburbs around the town centre, Kanahooka and Koonawarra, the work is knockdown-rebuilds, duplexes, granny flats and the first mid-rise apartment buildings. To the west, the West Dapto Urban Release Area is turning former farmland into new estates, with houses going up lot by lot next to families who have already moved in.

Both put building work very close to someone else's home. A dilapidation report records the neighbouring property's condition before work begins, so any later claim of damage can be checked against facts instead of memory.

What's being built

  • West Dapto estates. Wollongong City Council's West Dapto Urban Release Area is one of the largest growth areas in the Illawarra, with new roads such as the West Dapto Road upgrade and the Fowlers Road works opening up more land. Here the typical job is a new house, pool or retaining wall beside a recently completed home.
  • Town centre apartments. Mixed-use and apartment projects along the Princes Highway near Dapto Mall and the station are bringing basements and multi-storey buildings next to older shops, cottages and units.
  • Knockdown-rebuilds and dual occupancies in Dapto, Kanahooka, Koonawarra and Brownsville, usually with demolition and new footings close to the boundary. See our guide to dilapidation reports for demolition.
  • Pools and outbuildings across the area, where excavation and compaction sit right on the fence line. See pool dilapidation reports.

Ground and site conditions

Dapto sits between the escarpment and the western shore of Lake Illawarra, and the ground changes across that short distance. Low-lying land along Mullet Creek and its tributaries is part of a mapped floodplain, and council has bought back high flood-risk homes in the catchment over the years. On those flatter, wetter sites, slabs, paving and brick veneer can already show movement before any work starts nearby.

Further west the land rises into the hillier foothills below the escarpment. New estates there involve cut-and-fill, retaining walls and stepped lots, so a neighbour's retaining wall, fence footings and drainage are often as important to record as the house itself. In new estates we also pay attention to homes that are only a year or two old. Early settlement cracking is common, and it needs to be on record before the lot next door is excavated.

Housing stock we inspect

Dapto grew from a dairy and railway town, and its older streets still have weatherboard and fibro cottages near the town centre. Around them are post-war and 1970s–80s brick and brick veneer homes in Kanahooka, Koonawarra and Brownsville, villa and townhouse complexes, and the newer project homes of Horsley and the West Dapto estates. Each has its typical existing defects, from cracked fibro sheeting and sagging timber floors to cornice cracks and tile movement in newer homes, and a careful record separates those from anything new.
