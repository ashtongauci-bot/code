In the Inner West, building work usually happens right against a neighbour. Rear extensions, second storeys and new basements on terraces and semis mean digging and demolishing beside a shared wall, often on houses more than a century old.

{{PHOTO:concrete-retaining-wall-stairs|Concrete retaining wall and stairs in a narrow side passage|Side passages, stairs and boundary walls are recorded as carefully as the house itself.}}

That makes the dilapidation report especially important. Old lime mortar, shallow footings and decades of minor movement mean most Inner West houses already have cracks. Without a record, every one of them can become a claim.

What we focus on

  • Party walls, recorded from both sides wherever access allows, including roof space where the wall continues up
  • Ceilings and cornices in older homes, where cracking is common and often blamed on works next door
  • Rear lanes, courtyards and boundary walls, which take the brunt of access and excavation
  • Heritage detail in conservation areas such as fireplaces, decorative plaster and original joinery

Inner West Council frequently includes dilapidation conditions for adjoining properties. We'll check the wording and make sure each adjoining owner is covered.
